# WordLM candidate retrieval and selection fixes, 5 November 2026

The app now retrieves spelling alternatives from the trained WordLM vocabulary, including native-letter, titlecase and short grammatical forms. Previously, early input/valid-word checks and a dictionary-only candidate pool hid correct inflected forms from the ranker. A sentence-start frequency could also count as contextual evidence. Candidate diagnostics now distinguish actual lexical neighbours, bilateral evidence and source-context counts.

A new context component was fitted exclusively on the accepted train split: 3,287,357 sentences and 45,728,739 eligible tokens, with strict UTF-8 and zero replacement-decoded rows. It retains 7,841 orthographic profiles and 168,303 context features. Its posterior redistributes probability within a spelling family while retaining that family's original probability mass. It supplements the existing browser WordLM; it is not a replacement language model or calibrated correctness probability. Fitting read no QA, validation or test cases. Engineering count limits were fixed before evaluation.

The component is `lib/editor/wordlm/context-model.json`, SHA256 `5d7d592017ffa7417666d0a34f5ce472694befd35e146ba6ffbec45f44c295b4` (3,745,264 bytes, 947,854 gzip bytes). Its receipt is `context-info.json`; `scripts/train-wordlm-context.py` pins the accepted corpus, original WordLM, dictionary and builder hashes. Canonical train SHA256 is `b92e4edc6cb9eaaf214f35284cc8ec5e23acdb7763e40b281d8f0f9b9c9da8a9`. Retraining requires that separate source text; application inference requires only the committed counts and original WordLM. All 17 existing runtime JSON artifacts, including PunctCase weights and selection thresholds, retain their original bytes. Two new context JSON files are added.

Classified source morphology also covers present and negative verb forms, reported perfect, bare past participles and noun locative-relative forms. These verified surfaces support ordinary candidate retrieval, repeated-key repair and separating a glued conjunction after a finite verb. Training's feature/morphology contracts, active CharSpell source and accepted data remain unchanged.

The ranker preserves exact technical casing, proper names, explicit valid meanings, and verified structural decisions from preceding stages. Short words require bilateral context; overriding an explicitly valid native spelling requires decisive bilateral source evidence and the existing score margin. A unique source spelling may resolve an ungrounded morphological guess, but it cannot override unrelated reviewed or foreign technical decisions. A proposed early repeated-key pass was removed after it damaged valid doubled letters and names. No sentence-specific replacements or QA sentence lookup were added.

The frozen original 240 disagreements provide a development audit. On the final candidate, 87 of them match the existing references; 153 remain open. Frozen holdout matching moves from 395 to 396 out of 500, and all 2,000 no-harm cases remain correct, with no instability or exceptions in either set. These are engineering references, not independent real-user accuracy. Remaining ambiguities, unsupported spellings and upstream entity/morphology choices are recorded for further work; absence from a bounded model is not proof that the author's word is wrong.

Both WordLM and PunctCase continue to run automatically in text and mail. Model loading, candidate/context usage, known/scored tokens and PunctCase decisions are separate counters. Loading all selected weights does not mean every vocabulary word appears in a document or that every model proposal should replace the input. This audit does not attribute the measured spelling improvement to PunctCase or an unfinished CharSpell model.
